Independent reads of the slicing. ba-slice-story, attempt ba-attempt-008
Copied by hand, and shortened, from the readers' replies by the assistant that built this quest. No program wrote this file.

Who the readers were: four separate AI assistants (Claude), each started fresh. No person read the slicing. Each was told
to read only the files named to it, to run nothing and to write nothing. Those limits were given in words and not
enforced. None saw another's reply, and none after the first round saw an earlier version.

  Recounter   given the original and the first version of the slices, with the coverage table, the counts and the order
              table taken out of its copy. Asked to work the coverage and the order out by hand, with no program.
  Critic 1    given the three files of the first version. Asked, as a product owner, whether each slice is a slice.
  Critic 2    the same task, on the second version.
  Critic 3    given the third version. Asked for a verdict on each of the quest's first six criteria, and for every
              sentence that is false given the original's criteria.

The first version, as the recounter and critic 1 read it, is in review/.

==============================================================================
ROUND 1. The first version: 9 slices

THE RECOUNT, BY HAND. 20 criteria, 9 slices. Every criterion in exactly one slice: 20, none in none, none in two. Per
slice 6, 2, 1, 1, 2, 1, 1, 4, 2, which is 20. All 20 written-out criteria read identical to the original. Every "Depends
on" points backwards. No need in a later slice. Slice 1 needs nothing outside itself.
THE PROGRAM'S COUNT WAS THE SAME ON EVERY ONE OF THESE.
What it could not do by eye: rule out a difference that does not show, such as a different kind of space, or check the
checksum. What it noticed that the program does not look for: a sentence in the original that said "the first six
criteria" where the first slice was not the first six; and two "would notice" lines that claimed more than their criteria.

CRITIC 1. Its verdict: coverage and nothing-added hold. Value, not-a-layer and first-slice-alone "each fail in one place".
  - Slice 4, "a busy month in five seconds": "a quality attribute under a friendly name". A small shop notices nothing.
  - Slice 1 "ships a known-wrong sum". Its remedy was "a manager must be told", and "the telling has no channel": no
    criterion puts a warning on the page, and none may be added. It said to merge the refunds into Slice 1.
  - Slice 6's value, "what one product sold", is not delivered: the sum is of whole orders.
  - Titles and values said "this week" for "the last 7 days", and "only a manager can see" for what OC-02 refuses.
  - The order: the CSV file was seventh because "nothing before Slice 9 needs it", which "is a dependency argument, not
    a value one". The story's own stated want is a spreadsheet.
  - "Needs" lines: OC-11 named OC-04 where it needs OC-03. OC-14 cannot be tested without OC-15. OC-20 is "literally
    right, practically wrong": the same press of "Show" later does more.
  - The rejected slicing by layer is "a straw man". The tempting one is by the story's own seven area headings, and
    "the analyst's result still carries that residue".

WHAT WAS CHANGED AFTER ROUND 1
  Nine slices became seven. The refunds went into Slice 1. The speed criterion went into the slice that lets a manager
  choose dates. The CSV file moved from seventh to third. Titles and values were reworded. OC-11's "Needs" was corrected.
  A second rejected slicing, by area, was added, and the program now counts it: it passes coverage and order.
  A section was added listing what the original story does not say.

==============================================================================
ROUND 2. The second version: 7 slices

CRITIC 2. "A competent slicing with honest dependencies, but its claim that commentary promises nothing is false in
three places, and Slice 1 is not the thinnest honest first release." The counts were right: 8, 2, 1, 2, 1, 4, 2.
  - Slice 1's title said "truly", which the criteria do not earn: an order refunded in part is not handled, and the
    count includes refunded orders that the sum leaves out.
  - Slice 2's value said "any dates within 5 seconds". OC-20 covers one case, and the list only.
  - Slice 3 said the file "holds what the list shows". It does not carry "Refunded". It said a manager could work out
    "per-product figures" from it. The file has no product. "That false claim is the stated reason S3 precedes S4 and S5."
  - "Has to be shown to hold again", of OC-20, is an obligation no criterion contains.
  - OC-06 presses "Show", which only OC-05 introduces. Slice 5 therefore depends on Slice 2, and said Slice 1.
  - The rebuttal of the area slicing was stale: it still said the first release does not need refunds.
  - A thinner first slice exists: the list with refunds marked, OC-01 to 04 and 09, with the count and sum to follow.
  - It agreed with all three merges.

WHAT WAS CHANGED AFTER ROUND 2
  The false and overstated lines were reworded. OC-06 now needs OC-05, and Slice 5 depends on Slice 2. Six more gaps in
  the original were listed. The area rebuttal was corrected. The sentence saying the commentary promises nothing now
  says readers found lines that did.
  NOT CHANGED: the first slice. The thinner one is recorded in Slice 1 as an alternative that would be honest to release.
  It was not taken because it does not answer "what did it come to".

==============================================================================
ROUND 3. The third version

CRITIC 3. "The slicing is sound. No structural failure; one commentary line overstates what a slice gives."
  1. Each slice states its value          HOLDS WITH A RESERVATION: Slice 6 is thin by its own admission
  2. Cut by value, not layer              HOLDS
  3. Every criterion in exactly one slice HOLDS. Its own count: 8, 2, 1, 2, 1, 4, 2 = 20
  4. No slice depends on a later one      HOLDS WITH A RESERVATION: Slice 5 names Slice 2 and reaches Slice 1 through it
  5. The first slice can ship alone       HOLDS
  6. Nothing is added                     HOLDS WITH A RESERVATION: the criteria match word for word. The commentary
                                          invents detail, such as "two date boxes", and guesses at how often things are
                                          asked for
  Sentences it called false or unsupported: five. Slice 7's value said the email holds "the last 7 days' orders"; it
  holds the saved report's. Slice 4's reason said it was the first thing to tell a manager something new. Slice 1 said an
  order refunded in part "is counted in full", which the original does not say. A note said OC-18 alone sends an email
  "with nothing in it". Slice 1 said "a refunded order" where it meant one refunded in full.
  "Needs" lines it would argue with: five, and "none of these corrections would make a slice depend on a later one".

WHAT WAS CHANGED AFTER ROUND 3, AND NOT READ AGAIN
  Those five sentences were reworded. Nothing else: no criterion, no "Covers" line, no "Depends on" line and no "Needs"
  line. The program's counts are the same before and after.
  So the handed-in slices differ from the version critic 3 read by five sentences of commentary, which nobody has read
  since. Still standing from its report: the invented detail in the "would notice" lines, the guesses about how often a
  manager asks for what, Slice 6 being thin, and its five arguments about "Needs" lines.

==============================================================================
SUMMARY
  Coverage and order: counted by the program, by a reader by hand, and by two critics. All agree.
  Whether a slice is a slice: the first version was wrong in two places, a slice that was a quality attribute and a first
  slice that shipped a wrong sum. Both were found by a reader and not by the program, which passed that version.
  Commentary: each round of reading found lines that claimed more than the criteria give. Three rounds, and each found
  some. There is no reason to think the third found the last.
