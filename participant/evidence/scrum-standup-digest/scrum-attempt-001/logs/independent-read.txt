Independent reads of the digest and of its generator. scrum-standup-digest, attempt scrum-attempt-001
Copied by hand, and shortened, from the readers' replies by the assistant that built this quest. No program wrote this file.

Who the readers were: four separate AI assistants (Claude), each started fresh. No person read the work, and no teammate
was walked through it. Each was told to read only the files named to it, to run nothing and to write nothing. Those
limits were given in words and not enforced. None saw another's reply. The generator, its tests and its record were
finished and passing before any reply was read.

  Reader 1   given the first digest and gap report and nothing else. Asked, as a manager who will act on it, which lines
             do not answer "how do you know that?".
  Reader 2   given the first digest, the gap report and the records they were built from. Asked to check every
             statement against its cited record, by reading.
  Reader 3   given the generator as first built, the skill document, the first digest and the record of the evidence.
             Asked where the code and the written rules differ, and what input would make it assert something unsupported.
  Reader 4   given the final digest, gap report, skill document and records. Readers 1 and 2's tasks, on the final version.

ROUND 1. The first version

READER 2, AGAINST THE RECORDS. 31 statement lines. "23 verified from the citation alone. 8 not fully verifiable or loosely
worded, 0 flatly wrong." Every count was right. Every item of the context was in the digest or in the gap report: "None is
in neither."
The eight:
  - Two aging lines said "unchanged for 8 days" and "for 5 days". The records showed no change only up to the time they
    were retrieved, an hour and a half earlier. "It meets the stated rule, yet says more than the record shows."
  - A line said an item was blocked on the strength of a person's note, and cited the item's record, which says blocked
    is unknown. The note was not cited, and has no date.
  - A stale record named a run that was in no list of runs.
  - Three ages were cut short by 20 minutes, where every other age was given to the minute.
  - The removed item has no record to cite.

READER 1, THE DIGEST ALONE. It could not obey the title worded as an instruction, and found nothing about any person and no
personal data. What it would not accept:
  - The same aging lines: "It only reaches the 5-day threshold by assuming no change between retrieval and 09:00."
  - "OBSERVED lines state what a fresh record says, and nothing more" was untrue of the removed, count and aging lines.
  - A fourth inference rule was used under a heading that named three.
  - The digest said 4 stale records and the gap report said 5 stale.
  - It was too long to run a stand-up from, and had no summary at the top. One item appeared five times.
  - A title worded as an instruction "is still a live string for any downstream automated reader".

READER 3, THE CODE AGAINST THE RULES. The findings that mattered:
  - With two blockers, one done and one not, the generator printed that every blocker was done.
  - A record that called itself fresh was asserted even when no completed run was listed for it. A record's run was
    never matched against the list of runs.
  - A note with a slightly wrong heading or line was dropped without a word. So were two files claiming one item: the
    later one won.
  - Under the per-person scope, an item still in the context was reported as removed, "which is false", and a blocker
    outside the scope crashed the generator.
  - A status that changed where one side had not been asserted gave no change line and no gap.
  - A double quote in a title ended the quotation marking it as other people's text. A carriage return started a line.
  - A redaction name was matched inside ordinary words. A date in a title was taken for a telephone number.
  - The basis a record gives for being blocked was printed unredacted, and as "None" when missing.
  - A time with a month 13 crashed it. The previous state could be the file the run was about to write.
  - Of the check: it would pass a line saying "done" of an item in progress, if the citation was right. It would pass a
    digest with a fresh item left out, and a gap report with its entries deleted.
  - Of the record of the evidence: the test that no socket was opened could not fail, since the generator imports
    nothing that opens one. Several other checks tested the generator's own wording against itself.
  Its verdict on the twelve criteria: one holds, nine hold with a reservation, and two "hold on the sample, fail in
  general": gaps, and changes under the per-person scope.

WHAT WAS CHANGED AFTER ROUND 1
  The generator was rewritten. Aging is counted to the time the record was retrieved. A record is fresh only if the run
  it names is listed as completed and gives the same time. "Every blocker is done" is said only when every one is.
  Malformed notes, prose in notes, duplicate files and unreadable values each make a gap entry. The per-person scope no
  longer compares with a digest of another scope, and no longer fails on a blocker outside it. A status change with one
  side unasserted is a gap. Other people's text is redacted and made unable to end its quotation or start a line.
  Names are matched as whole words. The digest has a summary at the top, cites the note file, and lists a run that a
  record names and no list has.
  The check now also looks at the status word, the aging days, the age, the run, a fresh item left out, the count of
  work in progress and the number of gap entries.
  The record of the evidence: the socket run is now recorded as a formality, and the criterion rests on the list of what
  the generator imports and where it opens files. Checks that tested wording against itself were replaced by checks
  that work the answer out again from the records.
  Tests went from 54 to 83. Each fault above has a test, in a class named for the readers.
  NOT DONE: nothing detects a title that is worded as an instruction. That would mean reading titles for meaning.
