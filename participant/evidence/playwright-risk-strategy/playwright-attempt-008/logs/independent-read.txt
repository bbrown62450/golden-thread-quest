Independent reads of the test strategy and of its check. playwright-risk-strategy, attempt playwright-attempt-008
Copied by hand, and shortened, from the readers' replies by the assistant that built this quest. No program wrote this file.

Who the readers were: four separate AI assistants (Claude), each started fresh. No person read the work. Each was told
to read only the files named to it, to run nothing and to write nothing. Those limits were given in words and not
enforced. None saw another's reply. In each round the documents and the check were finished, and the check passing,
before any reply was read.

  Reader 1   given the first requirements and strategy, and the demo's code for background. Asked, as a test lead, to
             rebuild the ranking by hand, to judge each layer, and to find what was untested and not said to be.
  Reader 2   given the first check script, its two inputs and its log. Asked what wrong documents it would pass, what
             right ones it would fail, and which of its labels claimed more than they tested.
  Reader 3   reader 1's task, on the second version. Told what reader 1 found, and not to take the fixes on trust.
  Reader 4   reader 2's task, on the second version. Told what reader 2 found, and not to take the fixes on trust.

==============================================================================
ROUND 1. The first version: 15 risks, a check of 40 checks, all passing

READER 1, THE STRATEGY. The ranked table matched its own rebuilding from the stated levels, row for row.
  What it found:
  - The scales left choices open. Working from the clauses alone it put two risks at another level, and found seven
    more where either of two levels could be defended. There was no clause for code that is not written yet. The rule
    that the higher level is taken was stated for likelihood only.
  - Eight of the 15 risks scored 6, and seven of those were ordered by risk number alone. The requirements' must,
    should and could played no part.
  - Six "cheapest tests" could not be run against the shop. "Call the function that works out the amounts with a
    basket": the function takes no basket, is not exported, and loading its file starts the server. The demo keeps no
    orders and gives every order one reference, so the test for a repeated order would pass today with the feature not
    built. The demo accepts no code. Rounding is not in the sums at all.
  - Layers it would move: the narrow-screen check from a person to a browser test, because the reason given for a
    person was wrong; the keyboard part of the accessibility check to a browser test; the end-date decision down to
    unit.
  - Parts of requirements reached by no risk and not said to be untested: the money itself, in two must requirements;
    "by a second press"; the refusal message; a valid code wrongly refused.
  - The manual check was one "nobody would really do": no checklist, no screen reader named, no person.
  - Sentences that claimed too much: "fails for this fault and for nothing else"; "it has not changed", of a function
    the demo's own notes say was changed; a run-time comparison that was not like for like.
  The risk it would want a person to check even with a test: rounding, R-05. "The test's expected values are worked out
  by hand by the same person who reads the rule, so the test repeats that reading and does not check it."

READER 2, THE CHECK. What mattered:
  - A requirement added after a blank line under the table was never read, so the strategy need not mention it.
  - A risk written as a paragraph, or under a new heading after section 3, was invisible: no layer, no reason, no rank,
    and nothing reported.
  - A second layer line written "* Layer: UI" was not seen.
  - "Names who accepted it" was a list of twelve placeholder words. "TBC" or "someone" passed.
  - The rule for equal scores was fixed in the program and never compared with the rule the document states.
  - A risk nobody means to test can be relabelled "manual" and leave section 5 with no acceptance. The check cannot
    know.
  - A file with the other kind of line ending lost every field. A strategy with nothing untested failed.
  - Of the 40 checks, the eight in PART 3 and the one in PART 1 restated PART 2 and could not fail once it passed.
  - More than twenty rules had no trial. The last key of the ranking, the risk number, was exercised by nothing.
  - A line reading "- constructor: x" in a risk's block would crash it.

WHAT WAS CHANGED AFTER ROUND 1
  The strategy was rewritten. A clause was added for code not written. The higher level is taken on both scales. Equal
  scores are ordered by impact, then by the requirement's importance, then by risk number. Every risk gained a line
  saying what must exist before its test can be written. The narrow-screen check and the keyboard check moved to
  browser tests. Four risks were added, making 19, and the parts of requirements no test reaches are listed. The run
  times were worked out again from the cases each block lists.
  The check was rewritten. Anything written REQ-n or R-n anywhere must be a row or a risk. Every line of a risk's block
  must be one of its fields. The stated ranking rule must be the words the program works by. The one who accepted must
  be the one the document names. Line endings are made the same. PART 3 prints lists for a reader and makes no checks.
  Trials went from 29 to 72.
  Two acceptances given on the first version were kept. One risk's score rose from 1 to 2 under the new scales.

ROUND 2. The second version: 19 risks, a check of 75 checks, all passing

READER 3, THE STRATEGY. Section 2 matched its rebuilding, all 19 rows. Every count in the prose it checked was right.
  "The arithmetic and counts are right, but criteria 1, 6 and 7 are not fully met."
  What it found:
  - Two levels the scales themselves forced the other way. The payment risk, R-19, had a likelihood of 2 while its own
    block said the shop has no payment provider, which is the clause for something not written: 3, and a score of 9.
    A valid code refused, R-16, had an impact of 2 while R-02 rated the same loss, a tenth of the order, at 3.
  - The screen-reader check was "untested in practice with no acceptance". The document admitted nobody had done it
    and still gave it the layer manual. "Criterion 6 fails for it today."
  - "Several cannot be written yet": it counted them. Eleven of the sixteen planned tests could not be written against
    the demo.
  - R-09 is put in a browser while its own block says it could be read without one, against the strategy's own rule.
  - "Needs first" was incomplete for four risks. The test for R-16 could pass for a function that refuses every code,
    which was the very thing its reason said it guarded against.
  - Clauses reached by no test and not listed: "can be read" at 320 pixels; that an order exists; that two orders get
    two references; an expired code at the server.
  - Fifteen of the 19 risks sat in a group ordered by number alone. The document mentioned one such group of seven.
  - Section 8 said one more risk would need acceptance. If the release went without the unbuilt features, five would.
  The risk it would want a person to check even with a test: rounding, R-05, again. "The owner should sign the worked
  examples."

READER 4, THE CHECK. It traced each of reader 2's findings. Most were fixed. Two were fixed in part, and one only in
problems() and not in the rest of the program.
  What it found:
  - A requirement added in a second table with an identifier written "REQ-13a" passed. So did a risk written "Risk 20"
    or "R20", and field lines written before the first risk.
  - A second table that renders as a heading row alone was swallowed whole: a row could be shown to a reader and
    never read by the check.
  - The acceptor was compared only with a line of the same file. Changing both to "to be decided" passed.
  - Words written beside identifiers in a table cell were ignored.
  - The risk number was still not isolated as the last key of the ranking: the blocks are in number order and the sort
    keeps that order, so deleting the key changed no result.
  - With the other kind of line ending the main program still misread the files, though problems() did not.
  - Nine rules had no trial of their own. Five conditions could be deleted with every trial still passing.
  - Header sentences that claimed more than the code did: "however it is written"; "the person section 5 says".
  It verified by hand, against the code: the counts, the whole ranking, word counts for three risks, and all 72 trial
  outputs. "No log line disagrees with the code."

WHAT WAS CHANGED AFTER ROUND 2, AND NOT READ AGAIN
  The strategy: R-19, R-16 and R-02 now score 9, and R-12 scores 4. The clauses were reworded so that those are forced.
  The screen-reader risk, R-11, has no layer and is in section 5. The manual layer has no risk left. The document says
  that 10 of the 15 planned tests cannot be written yet, and names the 5 that can. R-09 is named as the one exception
  to the rule for choosing a layer. "Needs first" was completed for R-02, R-06, R-08, R-12, R-16 and R-17, and R-16's
  test has a case that must pass whatever is ruled. The clauses no test reaches are listed. All four groups ordered by
  number alone are named. Section 8 was rewritten.
  The check: identifiers in any spelling are looked for. Sections 2, 5, 6 and 7 must each have one table. Table cells
  that hold identifiers must hold nothing else. Nothing may be written in section 3 before its first risk. The acceptor
  must be the one the requirements file names. A trial moves a block to show that the risk number, and not the order
  of the blocks, is the last key. Trials went from 72 to 96, and each rule reader 4 listed as untried has one.
  Two acceptances were asked for again, because what was being accepted had changed: R-19, at its new score, and R-11,
  as untested.

  NOT DONE:
  - The check does not apply the rule that the higher level is taken. It checks that the rule is stated.
  - It cannot tell a risk that is missing, a requirement written with no identifier, or a "manual" nobody will do.
  - A sentence elsewhere in the document can contradict one the check has verified.
  - Reader 3's smaller points that stand: untested risks sit in the "do not need a browser" group; the bullet is still
    called "Cheapest test that would fail", which says more than a plan can; "tax rate changes rarely" is unsupported.

  NOBODY HAS READ THE HANDED-IN VERSION. The handed-in strategy differs from the one reader 3 read by everything in the
  list above: four scores, one layer, the ranked table, and about thirty sentences. The handed-in check differs from
  the one reader 4 read by several new rules and 24 new trials. The check passes on the handed-in documents, and that is
  the only thing that has looked at them since.

SUMMARY
  Can the ranking be rebuilt from the stated levels? Two readers did it by hand, one on each version, and both matched
  the table row for row.
  Can the levels be rebuilt from the scales? Not wholly. Each reader, working from the clauses, put some risks at
  another level, fewer the second time. The handed-in scales have not been tried.
  Was anything untested without being said? Yes, in both versions a reader found some. What they named is now listed.
  Did the check pass wrong documents? Yes, in both versions. Each form a reader named is now reported.
  A person has not read any of it.
