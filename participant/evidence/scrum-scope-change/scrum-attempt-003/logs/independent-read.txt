Independent reads of the scope-change report and of the program that writes it. scrum-scope-change, attempt scrum-attempt-003
Copied by hand, and shortened, from the readers' replies by the assistant that built this quest. No program wrote this file.

Who the readers were: three separate AI assistants (Claude), each started fresh. No person read the work. Each was told
to read only the files named to it, to run nothing and to write nothing. Those limits were given in words and not
enforced. None saw another's reply. In each round the program, its tests and its record were finished and passing
before any reply was read.

  Reader 1   given the first report and the snapshots. Asked to compare the two snapshots by hand, field by field, to
             add up every number, and to say which classifications it would argue with.
  Reader 2   given the first program, its rules, the snapshots and the first report. Asked what input would make it
             assert something the snapshots do not support, and where the code and the rules differ.
  Reader 3   given the second program, its rules, the snapshots and the second report. Both tasks. It was told what
             readers 1 and 2 had found, and not to take the fixes on trust.

ROUND 1. The first version (70 tests, 49 recorded checks, 43 planted bugs, all passing)

READER 1, BY HAND. "My classification agrees with the report's sections 2 to 6 for all 13 identifiers."
  Every number of section 1 matched its own sums: both totals of 30, the net change of 0, 9 added, 7 removed, the
  re-estimates of -4 and +2, and 22 points moved. Sections 8 and 9 matched. Every quoted entry and title matched the
  snapshots character for character. It could not see the run on identical snapshots.
  What it would argue with:
  - PIER-311 as noise. "Real extra work, uncounted, 0 points." Its description gained a sentence that asks for more.
    The section's heading said "nothing of the commitment differs", which claimed more than was shown.
  - PIER-309 as material. The words of one criterion are in another order and pass and fail the same tests. The report
    flags that it cannot tell, but the same sentence sits on an obvious change, 2 seconds to 10, and on this.
  - "Material means the commitment changed" contradicted the report's own admission. "May have changed."
  - "Remaining capacity" would be read as capacity left on the day. It ignores work done.
  - The section on who changed what "reads as naming people who overstepped, despite the disclaimer", and its one long
    line could not be parsed.
  - "Is in no count": the noise section has a count of its own.

READER 2, THE CODE AGAINST THE RULES. The shipped report agreed with the shipped snapshots. "The faults are all on other
inputs." The ones that mattered:
  - A snapshot name typed wrongly gave "Committed later: 0 points. Net change: -30 points", in bold, and exit code 0.
  - An estimate unusable at start and usable later put points into the net change that no part explained.
  - List entries written with no indent were dropped, and the item was listed as unchanged.
  - A stray "## " line inside a snapshot dropped every item after it, which were then reported as added.
  - A heading such as "### PIER-302 (was 12)" made the item both a gap and "added".
  - Sums of decimals gave "Net change: -1.11022e-16 points" beside "No change".
  - A number that was content, "24. December is excluded", was taken off as numbering.
  - The rule for setting formatting aside made eleven pairs of different meaning equal: "1,5 seconds" and "15
    seconds"; "status != active" and "status = active"; "-5 degrees" and "5 degrees"; a range and a pair.
  - Quotations cut at 157 characters could print two different texts as the same.
  - The day a snapshot was taken was printed outside quotes, so a file could put a sentence into the report.
  - Trouble in a snapshot that was not being compared stopped "No change" being said.
  - The check failed three kinds of correct report the program itself writes.

WHAT WAS CHANGED AFTER ROUND 1
  The program was rewritten. With a snapshot missing it says NOTHING WAS COMPARED and gives no total, and ends with an
  error code. An item is in the figures only if its estimate can be used on both sides, so the parts always add up.
  Sums are exact. Very little is now set aside as formatting: capitals, spacing, the kind of quote or dash, a mark at
  the very end, and order. A line that is not read keeps its item from being called unchanged or noise. Headings that
  are not what they should be are gaps, and the items under them are not called added or removed. Numbering is taken
  off only when it runs 1, 2, 3 down the list. Other people's text is quoted everywhere, and the report says when two
  quotations cannot show their difference.
  Wording: material "changed or may have". Noise "is a statement about three fields. It is not a statement that no
  work was added." Capacity "not yet committed", with "no account of work done". The last section says what
  `changed_by` records, one item to a line, and that it "is not a finding about anyone".
  Tests went from 70 to 105. Each fault has a test, in a class named for the readers.

ROUND 2. The second version (105 tests, 60 recorded checks, 59 planted bugs, all passing)

READER 3. "My comparison by identifier agrees with the report on every class, field, quoted text and number."
  Of the twelve earlier faults it traced: seven fixed, five partly.
  What it still found:
  - The formatting rule still made some different meanings equal. A run of full stops at the end was taken off, so
    "PDF, PNG, ..." equalled "PDF, PNG". A raised 2 was folded to a 2, so "10 squared" equalled "102". A straight
    double quote became a single one, so 6 inches equalled 6 feet. A tab was deleted and not treated as a space.
  - A list of one entry beginning "1." still lost its number.
  - A list that was emptied was "cannot be told", though the entries that went are known.
  - An item with a field given twice used the last value in the totals, with no mark on the net change.
  - A heading in other capitals, "### Pier-305", left the item reported as removed and in the bold net change.
  - An item that "cannot be told" showed none of its other differences.
  - Identical snapshots with the same gap in both did not report "No change". "The criterion fails."
  - A stray "## " line with no item under it still cut a list short without a word.
  - Entries quoted in the material section could still print the same on both sides.
  - The check passed a bold net change in a report that compared nothing, "NO CHANGE" in capitals, an item listed
    with a star, and a wrong estimate on an added item. It failed one kind of correct report.
  - The rules document said "nothing else is set aside" and "with both versions", and neither was true.
  - Wording: the capacity effects had signs opposite to section 1 with no word to say why. The noise section said
    the criteria "do not differ" above an item listed for its criteria. "Nothing below was estimated" in a report
    about estimates.
  It still thought the last section could be read as judging: three names stand under "other text" than the owner's.

WHAT WAS CHANGED AFTER ROUND 2, AND NOT READ AGAIN
  Formatting: only one mark at the very end is set aside; compatibility forms are no longer folded; a double quote
  stays a double quote; a tab is a space. A list of one entry keeps its number. A list emptied on one side is
  material. An item with a line not read or a field given twice is in no figure. A heading that cannot be read marks
  the net effect NOT COMPLETE. An item that cannot be told has its other differences printed. Items that are the same
  letter for letter are unchanged, so identical snapshots report "No change" and say that there are gaps. A heading
  that cuts a list short is a gap. Material entries that would print the same say so. Noise in a list shows both
  versions. The capacity lines say "of uncommitted capacity".
  The check: section 1 must read "Not compared" when nothing was; "no change" is looked for in any capitals; a line
  with any bullet must be an item; added and removed items must carry their own estimate and title; NOT COMPLETE must
  be there exactly when it should; identifiers are looked for with or without their marks.
  The rules document was corrected in each place the reader named.
  Tests went from 105 to 128, recorded checks from 60 to 68, planted bugs from 59 to 76.

  NOT DONE, and written into the rules document:
  - Capitals are set aside, so "5 mW" and "5 MW" are the same.
  - Reordering entries is noise, so a change in the order of steps is missed.
  - An entry nested under another is read as an entry of its own.
  - A line that starts "### " inside an item is read as a new item.
  - The last section still lists names under "other text". It says what is recorded, and what it does not say.
  - PIER-307, a change of meaning, and PIER-309, a rewording, still carry the same sentence. The program cannot tell
    them apart, which is the decision the participant made.

  NOBODY HAS READ THE HANDED-IN VERSION. The handed-in report differs from the one reader 3 checked by wording only: the
  noise section's opening sentence, the versions now shown for PIER-308, the words "of uncommitted capacity", "guessed
  or filled in", and the line on how to read noise. No class, no field and no number changed. The handed-in program
  differs by every code change above. Those are covered by tests and planted bugs, and by no reader.

SUMMARY
  Are the classes and the numbers right for these snapshots? Two readers compared the snapshots by hand, one on each
  version, and agreed with the report on every item, field, quotation and number.
  Did the first program assert things the snapshots did not support, on other input? Yes, in a dozen ways a reader found
  by reading the code. The second still did, in fewer and smaller ways.
  Does the handed-in program? Not in any way a reader named that was not then corrected or written down as a limit.
  It has not been read since, and each read so far found something.
  Which classification would a team argue with? Both readers of the report said the same one first: PIER-311, new work
  written into a description, which the rule calls noise.
  A person has not read any of it.
