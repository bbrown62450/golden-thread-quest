Can another person derive the same identity from the rules? context-stable-identities, attempt context-attempt-005
Written by the assistant (Claude) from the reports of two blind readers. No script made this file.

WHAT WAS DONE
  Two fresh AI assistants were started, one after the other. Neither had any memory of this work. Each was given two files
  and nothing else: rules.md, and a list of made-up source records with no answers. Each was told to read only those two
  files, to run no program and write no code, and to work each identity out by reading, as a person would with pen and
  paper. Where the rules did not settle a case it was to say so, and not guess.
  Their answers were then compared with what the skill gives for the same records.

WHAT THIS IS NOT
  The readers were AI assistants, not people. The limits were given in words and nothing enforced them: each said it kept
  them, and each said it ran no command at all. Both read the rules more carefully than a hurried person would.

ROUND 1: the rules as first written, 16 records
  Identities matching the skill: 15 of 16.
  The 16th was a record from a system the rules did not name, "asana". The reader wrote that the rules did not settle it,
  and that it would refuse the record but that this was its reading, not a stated rule. The skill refuses it. So the reader
  and the skill agreed on the outcome, and the rules had not said it.
  The reader found twelve gaps in the rules. The ones that could have led a careful person to a different identity:
    - nothing said what to do with a system other than the three;
    - "the field id" did not say the top-level id, and a Jira record also has a project id inside it;
    - a decimal was refused and a full stop was allowed, so the text "40003.0" could be read either way;
    - whole numbers only: negative numbers, exponents, null, true, false, lists and objects were not covered;
    - "letters" did not say whether an accented letter counts;
    - the fields the birth mark is read from were not named for Jira and GitHub, nor how to compare two times;
    - "ignore every other field" contradicted the sections that read the creation time and the previous identifier;
    - a rule for one batch and a rule for an earlier batch overlapped, and "batch" was not defined.
  One thing in round 1 was the assistant's fault and not the rules': it asked the reader to find three pairs of records
  that were the same item, and only two were in the list. The reader said so.
  The rules were then rewritten for every one of the twelve.

  The reader also found, from the rules alone, that records 1 and 6 are one item and that records 3 and 8 are one item,
  by their birth marks and not by their titles, and that record 9 is record 5 under a changed identifier, by the source's
  own statement and by nothing else.

  Where the reader said a careful person could still differ:
    - record 21. The rules described a Trello id as "24 characters". A reader could take that for a test and refuse
      "shortid". The reader did not, because the list of refusals has no such test.
    - record 17. The rules did not say how to tell text from a number.
    - records 16 and 18. "No identity" and "refused" could be read as two different things.
  And it pointed out a consequence the assistant had not seen: because text is never read for meaning, the TEXT forms of
  everything refused as a number are accepted. The text "-7" gives jira:-7. The text "40003.0" gives jira:40003.0, a
  separate and valid-looking identity beside jira:40003.

AFTER ROUND 2
  Five sentences were added to the rules for the points above: how to tell text from a number, where the system comes
  from, that "24 characters" describes and does not test, what a null previous identifier means, and what happens with no
  birth mark on either side. The last consequence was left as it is and written down as a limit.
  No third reader read the rules after those five sentences. So the rules as handed in are the round-2 rules plus five
  sentences that nobody has yet tried to follow.

WHAT EACH READER SAID ABOUT KEEPING TO THE LIMITS
  Round 1: kept them. Read the two files. Ran no command, wrote no code, wrote no file.
  Round 2: kept them. Read the two files. Ran no command, wrote no code, wrote no file. Said it did not count the
           characters of the Trello id by program, and that no identity depends on it.
