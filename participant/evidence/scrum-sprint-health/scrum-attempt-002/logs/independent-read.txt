Independent reads of the sprint health report and of its generator. scrum-sprint-health, attempt scrum-attempt-002
Copied by hand, and shortened, from the readers' replies by the assistant that built this quest. No program wrote this file.

Who the readers were: five separate AI assistants (Claude), each started fresh. No person read the work, and no teammate
was walked through it. Each was told to read only the files named to it, to run nothing and to write nothing. Those
limits were given in words and not enforced. None saw another's reply. In each round the generator, its tests and its
record were finished and passing before any reply was read.

  Reader 1   given the first report and the two snapshots. Asked to work every number out again by hand.
  Reader 2   given the first report and nothing else. Asked, as a team lead who will act on it, what it would not
             accept, and whether anything in it judges a person.
  Reader 3   given the generator as first built, the skill document, the snapshots and the first report. Asked where
             the code and the written rules differ, and what input would make it assert something unsupported.
  Reader 4   given the second report and the two snapshots, and not the code. Reader 1's task, on the second version.
  Reader 5   given the second generator, the skill document, the snapshots and the second report. Reader 3's task. It
             was told what reader 3 had found and that those faults were said to be fixed, and not to take that on trust.

==============================================================================
ROUND 1. The first version (78 tests, 50 recorded checks, 44 planted bugs, all passing)

READER 1, BY HAND. Sections 1 to 6 matched its own working exactly: every status count, every number of days, every
blocker, every count for a person, all seven risks with their reasons, and all eight gaps.
  What it found:
  - Section 7 did not report that the depends_on of FERRY-216 had changed, and did not say what it compares.
  - The gap for FERRY-220 blamed its updated date. Either of the two dates could be the wrong one.
  - A plural slip, and "left out of aging" said of items that are not started, where aging never applied.

READER 2, THE REPORT ALONE. The counts reconciled. It found no sentence that judged a person. What it would not accept:
  - The work-in-progress section put "1 more than the limit" beside a name without showing that two of those items were
    blocked by someone else's. "It implies what it says it does not say."
  - "6 days" was Wednesday to Tuesday: 4 working days. FERRY-209's "4 days" was Friday to Tuesday: 2 working days. It
    was aging only because of the weekend, and the report gave no weekday to see that by.
  - The seven risks are three situations. "Risk" is the report's own word.
  - Section 5 had no line for a wait where a rule could not be told.
  - "Recorded status: 22 of 24" was wrong of FERRY-222, whose status is recorded, as in_review.
  - Titles were shown for some items only, so the two titles the report warns about could not be seen.

READER 3, THE CODE AGAINST THE RULES. The findings that mattered:
  - A blank line inside the table silently dropped every row after it. So did a missing line of dashes: the first row
    was taken for it.
  - A threshold or limit that could not be used, such as "3 days", was reported as "none is set", with no gap.
  - Header lines were read from anywhere in the file, and the last one won.
  - "TBD" or "none" in the assignee column was counted as a person.
  - A wait on an item whose assignee or update date was blank was filed under "none of D1 to D4 holds".
  - A loop of three items or more was described as a chain.
  - The report printed the path of its snapshot, so its bytes depended on where the file was. The checksum was of the
    decoded text and not of the file's bytes.
  - Any cell containing "Made up" made the report call the snapshot made up.
  - Of the check: it could not fail on the generator's own output, and it passed many wrong reports.
  - Of the record of the evidence: several labels claimed more than their condition tested. One compared lists with a
    function that stops at the shorter. Some counts were written in by hand. The comparison with the limit was not
    worked out again. "Every age" was tested on one item.

WHAT WAS CHANGED AFTER ROUND 1
  The generator was rewritten. Every finding above was acted on, and each has a test.
  The report names its snapshot by the checksum of its bytes and prints no path. Header lines are read from one section.
  An unusable threshold or limit is a gap and is not called unset. A blank line does not end the table. Words that
  stand for nobody are gaps. Each age gives its weekday and how many of its days are a Saturday or Sunday, and items
  that are not aging are listed with theirs. Each count for a name says which of its items are blocked and which are
  aging. Section 5 has a list for waits where a rule cannot be told, a list of links that could not be assessed, and
  the number of different items waited on. Loops of any length are found. Section 7 says what it compares and what it
  does not, and compares depends_on. Every title is in a section of its own, and nowhere else.
  The record of the evidence was rewritten: each number is worked out again by its own code, the bytes are compared and
  not the decoded text, and a third run is made from copies under other names in another folder.

ROUND 2. The second version (125 tests, 75 recorded checks, 69 planted bugs, all passing)

READER 4, BY HAND. "Every count and list in sections 1-8 matches my own working."
  It worked out each age, each weekday and each count of weekend days itself, all seven waits, the five items waited
  on, each risk's reasons, the chain, the loop, the eight gaps and the five changes. All matched.
  It could not check the two checksums, or that the report reads nothing but the two snapshots.
  What it found:
  - Beside one name the report said "Aging: none". One of that name's two items has a blank update date. "This line
    presents the missing value as a 'no'."
  - "So none of them can be first", of the loop, is an inference about the work. The fields show each naming the other.
  - Section 7's list of what is not compared read as complete and was not: waits, risks, loops and gaps are not compared.
  - Section 2 does not show an item at exactly the threshold. Day 7 has none.
  - Wording: "0 days ... Of those days"; a gap whose field is given as "created and updated"; "left out of: aging" said
    of items that are not started; identifiers without their usual marks after "blocked_by is".
  Could be read as judging a person: over the limit and aging stand beside a name on one line, and the line does not
  say whose items the blockers are. The heading "for each person" sits against "it says nothing about any person".
  The numbers it said a team would argue with: "Waits that are a risk (7)" of 7, where any item not started makes a
  risk and one probable data-entry mistake makes two. "Aging (5)", for FERRY-209, aging only by the weekend. And "1
  more than the limit".

READER 5, THE CODE AGAINST THE RULES. It traced reader 3's seven faults and found them fixed, "but three of them survive
in other places". The sample report agreed with both snapshots on everything it re-derived.
  Wrong assertions and silent losses it found, each with an input that shows it:
  - Whether the item waited on is itself waiting could be untellable, and the wait was still filed under "none holds".
  - A header line written a little differently (two spaces, a star, a capital), or a section headed "The Sprint", gave
    "none is set" with no gap. Two sections headed "The sprint": the second was ignored.
  - An item whose status could not be used, with a blocker, was listed under "blocked, and not done".
  - A snapshot date that was recorded and unusable was called "not recorded".
  - In section 7, a threshold that had not been set was said to have been "not usable".
  - A bar inside a title made the row unusable, and the item was then said to be "not in the snapshot", and "removed".
  - A chain that an item in a loop waits on was never reported. A chain could end on an item in a loop.
  - With no threshold, section 2 showed no dates at all, against the skill document.
  - A created date after the snapshot was not a gap.
  - Words for nobody that are not on the fixed list, such as "TBC", are still counted as names.
  Crashes: a file that is not UTF-8; a chain of about a thousand items; a few dozen items each depending on many others.
  Of the check: it fails one kind of correct report (a previous snapshot naming an item that is in neither). It passed
  a changed number of risks, a changed threshold at the top, an age line with its weekday removed, and more.
  Text: the names of header lines were printed unquoted. Curly quotation marks and unseen characters passed through.
  A row not closed by a bar is quoted whole in its gap entry, title included.

WHAT WAS CHANGED AFTER ROUND 2, AND NOT READ AGAIN
  All three of reader 4's first three findings, and reader 5's wrong assertions and silent losses except the last.
  The count for a name now says which of its items cannot be told. The loop and chain sentences say only what is
  recorded. Section 7 lists everything it does not compare. Whether the item waited on is itself waiting can be "cannot
  be told". Header lines are read more tolerantly, only the six known names are read, and none or two header sections
  is a gap. An item with an unusable status and a blocker is listed apart. An unusable date is called unusable. "Not
  set" and "not usable" are kept apart in section 7. An unusable row is called that, and is not reported as absent,
  removed or added. Chains no longer include or depend on loop members. With no threshold the days are still shown. A
  created date after the snapshot is a gap. More quotation marks and unseen characters are replaced. The check knows
  the previous snapshot's unknown identifiers, and compares the numbers of waits and risks and the threshold at the top.
  The made-up snapshot of day 6 no longer describes a case that is only in day 7.
  Tests went from 125 to 146, recorded checks from 75 to 81, planted bugs from 69 to 85. Each fault has a test.

  NOT DONE, and written into the skill document as limits:
  - The list of words for nobody is fixed. Two names in one cell are one name.
  - A file that is not UTF-8, a very long chain or a very dense set of dependencies ends or exhausts the program.
  - The check's remaining blind spots, and the three ways it can fail a correct report.
  - A web address or an @name in a title is printed as it is.
  - Wording reader 4 named and that stands: "0 days ... Of those days"; "left out of: aging" for items not started; the
    gap whose field is "created and updated"; over the limit and aging on one line beside a name.

  NOBODY HAS READ THE HANDED-IN VERSION. The handed-in report differs from the one reader 4 checked by: three sentences,
  the added "cannot be told for" at the end of each count for a name, the added line in section 3 for items whose
  status cannot be used, the longer "Not compared" sentence, a few words at the top, and the two checksums. No count, no
  list, no age, no risk and no gap changed. The handed-in generator differs from the one reader 5 read by all the code
  changes above. They are covered by tests and planted bugs, and by no reader.

SUMMARY
  Are the numbers right? Two readers worked every number of the report out again by hand, one on each version. Neither
  found a wrong count, list, age or reason.
  Did the first generator assert things the snapshot did not support, on other input? Yes, in several ways a reader
  found by reading the code. None showed on the sample. The second generator still did, in fewer ways.
  Does the handed-in generator? Not in any way a reader named and that was not then corrected or written down as a
  limit. But it has not been read since, and each read so far found something.
  A teammate was not walked through the report.
