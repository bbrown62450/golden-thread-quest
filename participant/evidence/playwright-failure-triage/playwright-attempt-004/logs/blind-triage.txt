The blind classification of the six failures. playwright-failure-triage, attempt playwright-attempt-004
Written by the assistant (Claude) from the reports of three blind classifiers. No script made this file.

WHAT WAS DONE
  Three fresh AI assistants were started. None had any memory of this work. None was given the ground truth, the tests'
  file, the application, or the log of how the failures were made. Each was told to read only its own folder, to run
  nothing, and not to guess.

    Reader A and Reader B   each got its own copy of the six bundles, failure-01 to failure-06, and triage-rules.md.
                            They worked separately and did not see each other's answers.
    The first-look reader   got triage-rules.md and, for each failure, ONLY error.txt and screenshot.png. This is the
                            stretch goal: how often a classification changes after more evidence.

  The rules they used are the rules as they stood at step 5 of logs/sequence.txt. One paragraph was added afterwards.

WHAT THIS IS NOT
  The classifiers were AI assistants of the same kind as the one that planted the faults and wrote the rules. No person
  classified anything. The limits on what they read were given in words and nothing enforced them: each said it kept them,
  and listed the files it read. The bundles sit beside each other, so a reader could compare one failure with another,
  and both full-bundle readers did (for example, to see that a 404 for an image is in every bundle).

HOW OFTEN A CLASSIFICATION CHANGED AFTER MORE EVIDENCE (stretch goal)
  From the first look to the full bundle: 2 of 6 changed.
    failure-03   inconclusive -> test-defect    test-source.txt showed the read with no wait, and page-text.txt showed the page end up right
    failure-06   inconclusive -> environment    server.log and failed-requests.json named the refused connection
  4 of 6 did not change. For failure-04 more evidence did not help: it was inconclusive from two files and from fourteen.
  No classification changed from one class with a cause to another. More evidence turned "cannot tell" into an answer. It
  never overturned an answer. That is six failures and one reader, and is not a rate anyone should rely on.

WHAT WAS WRONG, AND WHO WAS WRONG
  No full-bundle classification was wrong. The quest asks for a failure first classified wrongly. These are the ones there are:

  1. failure-04, the first-look reader's forced bet: product-bug, about 40%, with environment at about 35%.
     The true cause is environment. Made to guess from an error and a screenshot, the reader guessed the wrong class. It
     had already put down inconclusive, correctly, and called the bet weak.

  2. failure-04, the assistant who planted it. The ground truth, written first, expected the bundle to show one thing: a
     slow answer with no cause. The bundle shows two. When the frozen server recovered, its two answers arrived in the
     order that sets off the page's race, and the page ended on "Order total: 0.00" above its lines. So the bundle holds a
     certain product bug as well, which the assistant had not planned and did not foresee. Both full-bundle readers found it,
     both said it was not why the test failed, and both said a reader could defend product-bug.
     The ground truth's sentence "a slow piece of application code would leave exactly the same evidence" is still true of
     the slowness. Its picture of the bundle as holding nothing else was wrong.

  3. Written down in advance in the ground truth as likely mistakes, and NOT made by anyone:
     failure-03 as product-bug, failure-02 as test-defect or flaky, failure-04 as environment, failure-01 as product-bug.

WHAT THE READERS SAID WAS MISSING OR MISLEADING (both, unless marked)
  - Nothing in any bundle can explain slowness: no timing of the server's steps, no record of its calls to the pricing
    service when they succeed, no machine load. That is the one gap that stopped a classification.
  - The rules did not say what to do when the product-bug list is met by a fault other than the one that failed the test.
    A paragraph was added for it afterwards.
  - A bundle's evidence is from the first attempt only. For a retry that passed there is a list of requests and no page.
  - The page's own script, which confirmed the race, is only inside trace.zip, and the rules' table did not say so.
  - "Customer: loading…" staying on the page in failure-01 and failure-06 looks like an unanswered request and is not. It is
    a small real fault of the page, which the assistant had not planted on purpose either.
  - failed-requests.json is mostly the harmless 404, three times in every bundle.
  - requests.txt is ordered by start and server.log by finish, so the same two requests are listed in opposite orders.
  - The second screenshot is identical to the first in four bundles and adds nothing there.
  - failure-04's later screenshot shows a wrong page the test never saw, and is easy to take for the reason it failed.
  None of these was changed in the bundles after the readers had read them.

READER A ON failure-04, WHOLE. The folder's path is left out. Nothing else was changed.
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- class: inconclusive
- cites: failure-04/error.txt, test-source.txt, requests.txt, server.log, failed-requests.json, api-responses.json,
  page-text.txt, screenshot.png, screenshot-after-wait.png, attempts.json, trace.zip (trace.network, the page script)
- reason: error.txt shows the test waited the full 2 s and the total was still "Order total: loading…". requests.txt and
  server.log show why: both data requests took about 3.2 s (3199 ms and 3211 ms) and were answered after the test gave up,
  both with 200. Nothing names a cause for the slowness. server.log has only the two plain 200 lines, failed-requests.json
  has only the promo.png 404s, console.log has nothing, and the response headers in the trace say nothing. By section 2 and
  the traps, slowness with no stated cause cannot be called environment. It is not a test-defect: test-source.txt shows a
  proper toHaveText wait and no faulty step, and the application did not end up right. So for the fault that made the test
  fail, no class's "must show" list is met.
- second fault, confirmed and separate: page-text.txt (moment 2) and screenshot-after-wait.png show that when the answers
  did arrive the page displayed "Order total: 0.00" above lines of 16.00 and 18.00, while api-responses.json says
  "total":34. This is the same race as failure-02: the summary answered 12 ms before the lines. It is a product bug in its
  own right, but it happened after the test had already failed, so it is not what failed the test (the "later screenshot"
  trap).
- still possible:
  - product-bug: the application itself stalled for 3.2 s. Both requests stalled together and finished 12 ms apart, and
    the server logged them as ordinary 200s.
  - environment: the machine or something the application depends on stalled. Nothing names it.
- what would settle it: evidence of what the server was doing during 01:42:50.36 to 01:42:53.56. That means server-side
  timing per handler step, any dependency call timing (for example the pricing service that failure-06's server.log shows
  the lines endpoint uses), and machine load (CPU, event-loop delay) for those seconds. If the stall can be reproduced with
  that logging on, the log line naming where the time went decides between product-bug and environment.
- confidence: medium. I am confident that no file states a cause for the slowness. I am less sure the rules intend
  inconclusive rather than product-bug here, because the bundle does prove a product bug, only not at the moment of failure.
